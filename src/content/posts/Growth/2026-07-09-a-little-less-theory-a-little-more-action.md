It feels like I'm stuck in second gear in the car _(to steal a phrase and potentially leave a handful of people scratching their heads)_. So I'm making the commitment today. A little less theory and a little more action. In particular, the feedback came in. I'm a great people person, but building the accountability and flow of a strong team (or org) is something that I still need to improve and enforce. I've known this for a while. As previously discussed the "ruinous empathy" I'm growing out of can often give me analysis paralysis or even a fear of doing the thing. There is still a high level of imposter syndrome that kicks in when the skills that got me this far in my career are now the skills that get in the way of me improving. The 20+ year seasoned vet should be able to perform better and it feels very clunky. 

So what's going on here? Why the declaration? Am I going to actually _do_ a thing about it _(oh look, I'm already making myself accountable)_? Well... yes and here's why and how...

## Orthodoxy means nothing without Orthopraxy

Whoops, big words there. Right living means nothing without right doing. Or potentially put a better way lots of great theory without actually putting words into action is meaningless. I can't "study well" into getting better practices in place. I actually have to do the practices and see if they work. If not, what good are they. Truth is, the "one shot" or "perfect game the first time" is nigh impossible, can sometimes be detrimental to finding something better and growing _(because if you did it perfect the first time then why go back to it?)_. It will also slow you down. This is where I find myself frequently. I'm so worried about getting the perfect thing out the first time that I will wait and refine and refine and refine and fret and when it launches it may be pretty good, but there are nevertheless things to fix and I would have done better to get the draft out early and gotten early feedback and small tweaks _(the 1% better)_ along the way. Just do the thing already! Sure don't do it blindly _(or maybe?)_ but don't wait for 100% ready. Don't wait for even 80% ready. I read an interesting post about learning and doing and unblocking yourself that said in effect to get 60% of your thoughts/learnings in place and then run with it. You'll get good feedback and interestingly enough your brain will naturally want to fill in the gap of your knowledge and help you move along even quicker.

## How risky is it really?

I've been on quite the creative tear this year. I revived my prayer app, rebuilt my breathing app, then created an agile pointing tool for work/devs in general and then put together a mini "news dashboard" of a slack feed at work that made some really interesting links and citations in a better format to read/consume. Not too shabby for a "try easier" year I started with not overloading myself. Aside from the tooling _(thank you Claude, Copilot, and Gemini/Antigravity, Firebase, Supabase)_ that made this go faster, the risk was so very low. Little money was lost. The time spent was learning regardless, and if people think it's lousy and don't use it there's a slight bruise to the ego but it motivates me a bit to make more improvements and food is still put on the table. So what's the risk with the "bigger" things I need to do at work? 

_It will be the wrong way to do it and everything will crash and burn_ - Wow, that second half is pretty intense, but you have a good team and peers that care for the success of things and will let you know if something is going awry. Besides, we can't improve if we aren't actually working on the thing to improve. Also, you have been in this industry for 20 some odd years now, you have broken enough things that you have a good sense of what not to do.

_I will get fired_ - Always legitimate, especially when taking care of family is the number one concern but you're at a place where inaction is more risky most times than "bad" action.

_People will lose respect for you because you've been in this industry forever_ - Oof, well there's a lot to unpack on that one. Are things that fragile where you are at that people would lose that much regard for you even if you are actively trying? If a few people do, can they really hold that much control over your life? No!

## What's it worth

Armed with that, let's just get out there and do it! There's so much to learn and tweak on when there's actually something in front of you (even if it is a bunch of paper outlining what you're putting into action) and you're not so reckless that you're going to explode the internet. Definitely don't "run fast and break things" but definitely ship that good thing and tweak it to make it great.

Circling back, I have some very practical things I'm implementing in my day to day to flex that accountability and flow muscle I need to be a better manager. I've set up a couple morning check-in routines _(thank you todoist)_ that I walk through now to escalate gaps, take action, and identify what's next to be proactive. I'm finalizing some new on-call and growth routines after feedback from the team and scheduling out the plan to make them happen, not just present it and hope everybody does it. It feels really awkward to start doing these, they're in that "70% complete" since I haven't seen if they work yet, but they will be out there and then I can tweak and grow.

...and shoot, this entire blog post was done within the hour on a train without the 3 days of machinations that go through my head about the perfect. It's a bit sloppy, but maybe it also sparks some conversation to make it better.
